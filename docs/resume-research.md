# Resume guidance for a software engineering candidate

Updated: 22 August 2026

## What the resume must accomplish

A resume is an interview-screening document, not a complete career archive. Its first page must let a recruiter quickly answer three questions: what role this person fits, whether they have the required skills and level, and what evidence shows they can deliver. Harvard describes the resume as a concise summary that should foreground the strongest assets and be tailored to the position. Microsoft confirms that its recruiting system uses job titles, skills, relevant experience, education, and resume content when aligning candidates with roles. [Harvard MCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/) [Microsoft Careers transparency FAQ](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/transparency.html)

For Vincent, the strongest default positioning is a product-minded full-stack software engineer who can take ambiguous problems through UX, implementation, and production delivery, with applied AI as a growing capability. The resume should prove that through selected work, not lead with a broad list of everything he has touched.

## Recommended structure

Use one conventional, single-column reading order:

1. Name and contact links
2. A two-line targeted summary, only if it clarifies the through-line of a varied career
3. Technical skills, grouped into a few factual categories
4. Experience in reverse chronological order
5. Selected projects only when they add evidence not already present in experience
6. Education

Microsoft explicitly recommends reverse chronological jobs, descriptive industry-standard job titles, projects nested under the job where the work occurred, and a separate skills section because parsers identify those skills more reliably. Penn recommends moving education below experience after graduation and says projects are especially useful on software engineering resumes when they demonstrate skills in use. [Microsoft Careers transparency FAQ](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/transparency.html) [Penn Career Services](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/)

Avoid a sidebar, columns, cards, skill bars, icons used in place of text, photos, tables, text boxes, and contact information in a header or footer. Greenhouse lists all of these patterns, especially columns, graphics, tables, headers, footers, and text boxes, as common causes of partial or failed parsing. Its parser also rejects image-only resumes and files over 2.5 MB. [Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)

## Experience bullets

Each bullet should make a compact claim in this order:

**Action + what was built or changed + why it mattered + result or scope**

MIT calls this the PAR pattern: Project, Activity, Result. It recommends beginning with a strong action verb, reflecting relevant language from the job description, and showing the outcome. Because recruiters initially scan, MIT also recommends bullets instead of paragraphs and keeping each bullet to one or two lines. [MIT CAPD on writing about skills](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes-writing-about-your-skills/)

Practical rules:

- Give the newest and most relevant roles the most space. Compress older or less relevant work.
- Use two to four high-signal bullets for a substantial recent role. Fewer are fine for older roles.
- Lead with ownership verbs such as built, designed, shipped, led, improved, migrated, or automated. Avoid openings such as “responsible for,” “worked on,” “helped,” or “assisted.” Penn likewise recommends direct action verbs and accomplishments. [Penn Career Services](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/)
- Quantify only when the number is known and defensible. Useful measures include users, transactions, revenue, conversion, latency, reliability, time saved, team size, release frequency, or delivery time.
- When hard metrics are unavailable, show honest scope: the workflow replaced, systems integrated, production constraints handled, stakeholder group served, or feature owned from discovery through release.
- Put relevant technologies in the bullet where they prove how the result was achieved. Keep the skills section as a fast index, not a keyword dump.
- Preserve enough context for a reader outside the company. Microsoft advises avoiding employer-specific terminology and using broadly understood technical terms and titles. [Microsoft Careers transparency FAQ](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/transparency.html)
- Never invent outcomes. A modest, specific, verifiable bullet is stronger than an inflated metric that cannot survive interview follow-up.

## ATS compatibility and tailoring

Submit a text-based PDF or DOCX, following the employer's requested format. Use common fonts, clear standard headings, consistent dates, and ordinary text. MIT recommends avoiding text boxes, tables, colors, and stylized fonts, then testing the result by converting it to plain text to confirm that the reading order and content survive. [MIT CAPD 2026 resume resources](https://capd.mit.edu/blog/2026/06/23/interphase-2026-resume-resources/) [MIT ATS guidance](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/make-your-resume-ats-friendly/)

Tailoring should be truthful alignment, not keyword stuffing:

- Start from the role's required qualifications and recurring language.
- Reorder skills and bullets so the most relevant evidence appears first.
- Use the employer's standard terminology where it accurately describes the work.
- Keep accurate, complete job titles and dates.
- Check the parsed application profile after upload. Microsoft warns that parsers can miss information and says skills and titles materially affect recommendations. [Microsoft Careers transparency FAQ](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/transparency.html)

The practical operating model is one comprehensive source resume plus a small number of focused variants, such as product-focused full-stack, backend/integrations, and applied AI systems. Penn recommends maintaining a long-form source and editing it down for each target; MIT notes that tailoring helps both ATS matching and the human reader see the connection. [Penn Career Services](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/) [MIT CAPD 2026 resume resources](https://capd.mit.edu/blog/2026/06/23/interphase-2026-resume-resources/)

## Recruiter scan behavior

Design for a fast first pass. MIT says recruiters spend only a few seconds on average and cites less than six seconds as a reason to keep the strongest, most relevant information on page one. The useful conclusion is not to optimize for a literal timer, but to make role, level, core stack, recent employer, and strongest evidence visible without hunting. [MIT CAPD on writing about skills](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes-writing-about-your-skills/) [MIT CAPD 2026 resume resources](https://capd.mit.edu/blog/2026/06/23/interphase-2026-resume-resources/)

That means:

- Use familiar section names such as Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, and Education.
- Make company, title, dates, and location visually distinct and consistent.
- Put the strongest clause at the start of each bullet.
- Preserve white space and a readable 10 to 12 point font rather than squeezing in weak content.
- Ensure page one can stand alone even if the reader never continues.

## Links

Include email, phone, location, LinkedIn, GitHub, and portfolio only when each destination is current and useful. Penn specifically recommends LinkedIn and, for software roles, GitHub or a personal site. Microsoft says GitHub details on a resume can be attached to a candidate profile and used as recruiter-visible information. [Penn Career Services](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/) [Microsoft Careers transparency FAQ](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/transparency.html)

For reliable parsing and human use, render short recognizable URLs as actual text and hyperlink the same text, for example `github.com/username`, not an icon or an opaque “click here.” This is a conservative implementation choice based on Greenhouse's warning against graphics and hidden or complex layouts. Every link should be tested in the exported PDF.

Projects should link only to strong evidence: a live product, focused case study, credible repository, or concise walkthrough. A project earns resume space when it demonstrates a target skill or outcome that the experience section does not already establish.

## One page or two pages

Use one page as the default application resume. MIT recommends one page because the second page can bury the strongest evidence and dilute relevance, while acknowledging that two pages are not inherently wrong. Its checklist allows two pages for advanced degrees or extensive experience around 10 or more years. [MIT CAPD 2026 resume resources](https://capd.mit.edu/blog/2026/06/23/interphase-2026-resume-resources/) [MIT resume checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/)

For Vincent, calendar length alone does not justify page two. Use two pages only for a senior or lead role when the additional page contains distinct, role-relevant evidence such as architecture ownership, team leadership, production scale, or major outcomes that cannot fit legibly on page one. Do not shrink below roughly 10 points or compress white space merely to preserve one page. If a second page exists, page one must still carry the complete positioning and strongest proof.

## Recommended decision for the current resume

Build a focused one-page master for product-oriented full-stack software engineering roles. Prioritize the most recent product engineering experience and a small number of strong shipped systems. Keep applied AI present as credible implementation experience, not as a sweeping identity claim. Reduce early-career entries to title, company, dates, and at most one relevant proof point. Retain a longer source document privately so targeted two-page versions can be produced when a particular senior role genuinely requires more evidence.

Before release, perform four checks:

1. Read the first third for a clear role and level in ten seconds.
2. Extract the PDF text and verify its order, section names, dates, and links.
3. Match every claim against LinkedIn, the portfolio, or another defensible source.
4. Tailor the skills and first bullets to a real job description before judging whether the resume is effective.
